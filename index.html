Hooks and When to Use Them

Ten proven hook types to stop the scroll in the first second — each with a definition plus a real example for Accommodation (ACC) and Things-to-Do (TTD) content.

1 Audience Call-Out▸

Call out a specific viewer identity, desire, or situation, then ask them to stop scrolling.

ACC

"If you're planning a beach vacation but hate crowded resorts, stop scrolling. This beachfront hotel might be the quietest stay I've found."

TTD

"If you're visiting Orlando with kids under 10, stop scrolling. This theme park area saved our whole day."

2 Best Way / Best Place▸

Open with "the best way to…" or "the best place for…" and immediately promise a practical recommendation. Works when the video helps viewers make a choice.

ACC

"The best way to book this resort is not the cheapest room. It's this ocean-view category, and here's why."

TTD

"The best way to do this theme park in one day is to start at the back, not the front."

3 Curiosity Discovery▸

Start with a surprising discovery, hidden feature, or "I can't believe…" line — a "never seen anything like this" suspense builder.

ACC

"I can't believe this hotel hides its best view behind the spa hallway. Most guests walk right past it."

TTD

"I can't believe this attraction has a secret entrance that cuts the wait almost in half."

4 Problem + Solution▸

Name a pain point first, then promise the solution. Includes "Do you have problems with ___?" and "Here's the perfect solution."

ACC

"Do you hate resorts where every good chair is gone by 8 a.m.? This hotel solved that problem in a surprisingly smart way."

TTD

"Do you hate wasting half your theme park day in lines? This is the route I'd use if I only had one day."

5 Mistake / Red Flag Warning▸

Open with a mistake, red flag, or warning: "Don't make this mistake," "red flags to look for," "you need to stop doing this."

ACC

"Don't book this hotel before you check where your room faces. The difference between these two towers is huge."

TTD

"Don't make this mistake when visiting this theme park: arriving at opening time is already too late for this ride."

6 Listicle Promise▸

Lead with a numbered list that promises quick value: "5 restaurants that belong on your bucket list," "3 things to do before…"

ACC

"Three things I wish I knew before staying at this beachfront resort."

TTD

"Five attractions inside this park that are actually worth the wait."

7 Visual Motion Hook▸

Use a physical action in the first second: placing the phone down, sliding an object into frame, entering the scene, or sudden movement.

ACC

Slide the room key card across the screen, then cut to the balcony: "This key unlocked the best sunrise view I've had all year."

TTD

Start with a fast whip-pan from the park map to the ride entrance: "This is the first ride you should do when the gates open."

8 Show the Result First▸

Begin with the payoff before explaining how or why — give viewers a reason to stay before the setup.

ACC

Show the infinity pool at golden hour first: "This is why I'd come back to this resort, even though the room had one problem."

TTD

Show the final drop, fireworks, or best view first: "This moment made the 45-minute wait worth it."

9 Question Hook▸

Ask a direct question that creates a knowledge gap: "Did you know…," "What would you do if…," "Is it just me, or…"

ACC

"Did you know this resort has two beaches, but only one is good for swimming?"

TTD

"Is it just me, or is this the most underrated ride in the entire park?"

10 I Tested It So You Don't Have To▸

Position the creator as the tester who saves the viewer time, money, or risk: "I tried every ___ so you don't have to."

ACC

"I tried the cheapest room, the buffet, and the beach club at this resort so you don't have to guess what's worth paying for."

TTD

"I tested every paid add-on at this theme park so you know what to skip and what to buy."

Ask Yourself These Questions Before Posting

Run through this quick checklist before hitting post — each question catches a common reason a video underperforms.

QuestionIf No…Fix It
Can I name the hook type in 3 seconds?The angle is probably unclear.Rewrite the opening around one hook type.
Do I know who this video is for?The viewer may not feel called out.Add audience language: families, couples, tourists, locals, budget travelers.
Does every claim have proof?The video may feel like an opinion.Add footage that proves the room, ride, food, price, route, or booking path.
Does the CTA match the angle?The viewer may watch but not act.Use save/book/check dates/tap POI based on the video job.
Could this be split into two posts?The video may be trying to do too much.Turn each promise into its own hook-angle post.

Recommended Workflow (Pre · During · Edit)

A simple four-phase system to plan, shoot, and edit content that actually converts — from research to final cut.

1 Before You Go▸
  • Pick the vertical
  • Save examples you want to model
  • Write 5–10 hook angles
  • List proof needed for each angle
  • Plan CTA for each post
2 While You're There▸
  • Check gaps before leaving
  • Record quick reactions
  • Get comparison shots
  • Capture the payoff first
  • Film proof by category
3 Build Each Video Around One Promise▸
  • Cut anything that does not support the promise
  • Make the CTA match the angle
  • Use one hook type only
  • Start with the strongest visual
4 Avoid These Traps▸
  • One generic recap
  • Same opening for every post
  • Pretty footage with no booking reason
  • CTA that appears too late or is unclear
